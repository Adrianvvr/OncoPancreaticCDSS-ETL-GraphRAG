## Introduction

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is the seventh highest cause of cancer-related deaths globally and the third major cause of cancer-related mortality in the United States. Pancreatic cancer is characterized by non-specific symptoms and gradual development, thus only around 15% of individuals with the disease are candidates for surgery following a diagnosis.. Furthermore, less than 10% of individuals identified with the advanced disease have a 5-year survival rate. Despite the promise of emerging therapies, Gemcitabine (GEM) -based combination chemotherapy strategies continue to be the treatment of choice for advanced PDAC.. Patients frequently experience improvements in their condition after receiving chemotherapy during the early stages of treatment; nevertheless, the effectiveness of this treatment regimen is severely constrained by the development of drug resistance.. Therefore, understanding the fundamental processes that underlie treatment resistance in pancreatic cancer is crucial for improving the prognosis of PDAC patients.

Chemotherapy resistance is driven by multiple factors, particularly the tumor microenvironment (TME), which has recently attracted widespread attention. The promotion of tumor progression and induction of drug resistance by mesenchymal cells in TME have been validated in a variety of tumors. Pancreatic cancer is undoubtedly one of the solid tumors with the highest mesenchymal content. Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) are remodeled from normal fibroblasts (NFs) by cancer cells and constitute the majority of cells in the stroma of pancreatic cancer.. CAF-induced fibrous hyperplasia can directly lead to decreased drug perfusion, thereby reducing the effect of chemotherapy. Recently, CAFs from tumor tissues were found to change TME to promote chemotherapy resistance. However, the results of these studies were affected by tumor heterogeneity, and clinical trials of anti-CAF therapies have not achieved satisfactory efficacy. This raises questions regarding the true role of CAFs in tumor microenvironment.

Iron is required for the process known as ferroptosis, which is a form of controlled cell death because of excessive buildup of lipid peroxidation. Its molecular properties distinguish it from other types of controlled cell death.. The progression of a variety of diseases, including chemotherapy resistance, is associated with the dysregulation of ferroptosis. Long-chain fatty acid-CoA ligase 4 (ACSL4) plays a central role in ferroptosis and positively regulates this process by activating long-chain fatty acid metabolism. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are a group of non-protein-coding RNA transcripts with a length of 20–22 nucleotides. miRNAs can bind to protein-coding messenger ribonucleic acid (mRNA) to degrade or interfere with protein translation, thereby regulating metabolism and tumor progression. Recent studies have shown that miRNAs in exosomes can be efficiently transmitted between cells and induce tumor drug resistance and progression through different mechanisms. CAFs are the predominant source of exosomes within the microenvironment of pancreatic cancer. The mechanism of exosome-derived miRNAs in regulating drug resistance in pancreatic cancer requires further study.

In this study, we collected primary human CAFs and NFs to explore the impact of fibroblasts on GEM resistance in pancreatic cancer as well as the mechanisms that underlie this resistance. Our study suggests that CAFs can secrete exosomes that highly express miR-3173–5p in a system that mimics GEM chemotherapy. After ingestion by PDAC cells, exosome-derived miR-3173–5p-targeted ACSL4 to mediate GEM chemoresistance by inhibiting ferroptosis.

## Results

### Isolation of primary human pancreatic CAFs and exosomes

First, we obtained NFs and CAFs from normal paracancerous pancreatic tissues and PDAC tumors, respectively. None of the enrolled patients had received chemotherapy treatment. The isolated NFs and CAFs grew well, and the cells showed typical fibroblast morphology (fusiform, fascicular, and scattered). On the basis of immunofluorescence analysis, there was not a discernible distinction between CAFs and NFs in terms of the expression of fibroblast markers such as α-SMA, vimentin, or FAP in any of the cultivated cells. To further verify that fibroblasts were not contaminated by PDAC cells or immune cells, we performed an immunohistochemistry (IHC) analysis. By using IHC, we were able to establish that the culture did not include any cells that expressed the endothelium marker CK19 or the immune cell marker CD45. On the other hand, matched sections of primary tumors had cells that expressed both CK19 and CD45.

In order to investigate the impact of fibroblast-derived exosomes on the GEM resistance of PDAC cells, exosomes were isolated from the conditioned media (CM) by ultracentrifugation and then detected by transmission electron microscopy and nuclear transfer amplification (NTA). The exosome marker proteins CD9 and CD63 were positive in the isolated exosomes. To verify that exosomes secreted by CAFs were ingested by PDAC cells, exosomes were prestained with PKH26 (red). In PDAC cells, red fluorescence was detected using laser scanning confocal microscopy following incubation for a period of 12 h. These results indicated that the exosomes were successfully ingested by PDAC cells. The same validation was performed for NF exosomes.

### CAFs secreted exosomes to inhibit ferroptosis in PANC-1 and BxPC-3 cells following GEM treatment in a non-contact co-culture system

Because chemoresistance to GEM develops in initially sensitive tumors, usually within a few weeks after treatment, we speculated that a novel drug resistance mechanism might develop in the TME after chemotherapy. Exosomes are critical regulators in the PDAC microenvironment, and are released by mesenchymal cells. Therefore, we investigated the exosomes derived from CAF-and NF in GEM-mediated chemoresistance of pancreatic cancer cells. To simulate the TME of PDAC cells following treatment with GEM chemotherapy, we established a non-contact co-culture system and treated the cells with GEM. The natïve, co-induced, and independently induced fibroblast exosomes were extracted; the experimental process is shown via a flowchart. Importantly, all GEM-treated CAFs and NFs underwent a 72-h standard culture before exosome isolation to ensure that the exosomes originated from stabilized NFs or CAFs. The findings demonstrated that exposure of PANC-1 and BxPC-3 cells to exosomes originating from CAFs or NFs did not result in enhanced ability of the cells to survive GEM treatment. Similarly, native exosomes did not ameliorate the increase in intracellular Fe2+ and lipid ROS levels in PANC-1 and BxPC-3 cells caused by GEM treatment. PANC-1 and BxPC-3 cells were subsequently co-incubated with exosomes produced from CAFs and NFs that were co-induced with PDAC cells using a non-contact co-culture technique. According to the findings, the presence of co-induced CAF exosomes dramatically increased the number of surviving PANC-1 and BxPC-3 cells. and ameliorated the rise of intracellular Fe2+ and lipid ROS levels after GEM treatment, whereas co-induced NF exosomes did not. This finding indicated that the co-induced CAF exosomes promoted ferroptosis resistance in PDAC cells. To verify whether CAFs are independently induced to secrete resistant exosomes in the absence of tumor microenvironment, we incubated the independently induced exosomes with PANC-1 and BxPC-3 cells. Cell survival, intracellular Fe2 + levels, and lipid ROS levels were measured, and the findings indicated that there was no statistically significant difference between any of the experimental groups. Due to the fact that the patterns seen in the PANC-1 and BxPC-3 cell lines were parallel to one another, we decided to conduct the remaining tests using solely PANC-1 cells.

### MiR-3173-5p was upregulated in co-induced CAF exosomes and directly targeted ACSL4

The regulatory activity of exosomes is mainly due to the large quantities of encapsulated miRNAs. To identify the key regulatory miRNAs in exosomes, we analyzed the miRNA expression profiles of native vs. co-induced CAF exosomes. Results showed that the level of a series of miRNAs changed significantly. We then analyzed the targeted miRNAs of ferroptosis-positive regulators using the TargetScan database. As shown in the Venn diagram, miRNAs miR-3173–5p and miR-675–5p were chosen as prospective targets to investigate. In order to confirm that miR-3173–5p and miR-675–5p were overexpressed in co-induced exosomes in comparison to native exosomes, RT-qPCR was carried out. Then, Western blotting was used to detect the predicted ferroptosis-related target proteins of miR-3173–5p (ACSL4) and miR-675–5p (ALOX15) in PANC-1 cells treated with GEM and incubated with native and co-induced exosomes, respectively. The results showed that the co-induced CAF exosomes reduced the expression of ACSL4 in PANC-1 cells, whereas the expression of ALOX15 was not statistically different. Therefore, miR-675–5p was excluded. To further elucidate whether ACSL4 is a target gene regulated by miR-3173–5p, luciferase analysis was carried out. According to the findings, the miR-3173–5p mimic had a considerably inhibitory effect on luciferase activity in the WT group but had no such effect in the MUT group. RT-qPCR was carried out in order to get additional insight into whether or not PDAC cells are capable of ingesting fibroblast-derived exosomes, which would lead to an increase in the level of miR-3173–5p expression in PDAC cells. Following incubation with co-induced CAF exosomes, the results indicated that there was a discernible increase in the expression of miR-3173–5p in both PANC-1 and BxPC-3 cells.

### The level of miR-3173-5p was elevated in tumor tissues and further upregulated in tumor tissues following chemotherapy

Excavation of data deposited in TCGA revealed that the expression of miR-3173–5p was elevated in pancreatic cancer in comparison to the neighboring tissues. Additionally, in tumor tissues, the expression of ACSL4, which is a target of miR-3173–5p, was suppressed. Analysis of the ROC curve revealed that a higher expression of ACSL4 was linked to a lower 5-year survival rate in patients with pancreatic cancer. These results implied that miR-3173–5p potentially promote the development of cancer. To further verify the expression of miR-3173–5p in pancreatic cancer following chemotherapy, RNAs were extracted from paraffin blocks of patient specimens. The administration of chemotherapy led to a rise in the relative expression of miR-3173–5p in pancreatic cancer tissues, as determined by RT-qPCR.

### MiR-3173-5p upregulated ferroptosis resistance in PANC-1 cells by inhibiting ACSL4

PANC-1 cell lines with miR-3173–5p overexpression (lv-pre-miR3173–5p) and silencing (lv-antago-miR-3173–5p) were established using lentiviral vectors. This was done in order to validate the effect of miR-3173–5p on GEM-resistant and ACSL4-expressing cells. RT-qPCR was applied to confirm the reliability of the regulatory impact brought about by lentiviral transfection. According to the findings, there was a correlation between the increased expression of miR3173–5p in PANC-1 cells and a reduction in the levels of mRNA and protein expression of ACSL4. Moreover, upregulation of miR-3173–5p significantly improved PANC-1 cell viability and reduced intracellular Fe2+ and lipid ROS levels following GEM treatment, implying that ferroptosis was inhibited. Similarly, decreasing intracellular miR-3173–5p expression increased mRNA and ACSL4 protein expression, promoting ferroptosis in PANC-1 cells. We assessed the IC50 of GEM in transfected lv-pre-miR-3173–5p and lvantago-miR-3173–5p cells in order to further validate the influence of miR-3173–5p regulation on the sensitivity of PANC-1 cells to GEM. The findings demonstrated that an increase in miR-3173–5p levels resulted in a considerable decrease in the sensitivity of PANC-1 cells to GEM.

.

Silencing ACSL4 with siRNA allowed us to investigate the biological properties of ACSL4 in PDAC cells, which enabled us to evaluate whether or not ACSL4 is a downstream target and regulator of miR3173–5p for its role in the control of ferroptosis. First, we verified that the vehicle controls had no effect on ACSL4 protein expression and cell proliferation in PANC-1 cells. Thereafter, we generated three different siRNAs to suppress the expression of ACSL4 and selected the siRNA that had the most potent impact, which was confirmed by Western blotting. The investigation revealed that the transfection of si-ACSL4 led to an increase in the vitality of PANC-1 cells., while intracellular Fe2+ and lipid ROS levels were decreased. This implied that ferroptosis resistance was enhanced. More importantly, antago-miR-3173–5p-mediated ferroptosis sensitivity was partially reversed. These findings suggested that miR3173–5p was responsible for promoting ferroptosis resistance in PDAC cells by sponging ACSL4.

### miR-3173-5p promoted GEM resistance in PDAC cells in a xenograft tumor mouse model

We utilized lentiviral vectors that contained short hairpin RNA (shRNA) to establish miR-3173–5p-overexpressing and -knockdown cell lines in order to investigate the impact that miR-3173–5p on PDAC in vivo. In order to construct the xenograft tumor models, these generated cells, as well as PANC-1 cells that had been transfected with a vehicle, were administered subcutaneously into the armpits of nude mice. Prior to GEM or saline therapy, the results revealed no statistically significant differences in tumor volume among the groups., implying that miR-3173–5p does not promote tumor growth in vivo. Compared with the vehicle, the average weight and volume of the tumors were statistically larger in the pre-miR-3173–5p group and significantly smaller in the antago-miR-3173–5p group after 15 days of GEM treatment. Western blot analysis revealed that the overexpression of miR-3173–5p was correlated with the downregulation of ACSL4, while the converse was found in the antago-miR-3173–5p group. RTqPCR showed that the ferroptosis marker, PYGS2, was upregulated in the pre-miR-3173–5p group, and there was no significant difference in CASP3 (an apoptotic marker) between all GEM treatment groups. This result indicated that the increase in GEM resistance caused by miR-3173–5p was not related to the regulation of apoptosis. Interestingly, we discovered an increase in intra-tumoral Ki-67 expression that correlated with the amount of miR-3173–5p expressed in the GEM-treated group. These data indicated that miR-3173–5p sponged ACSL4, promoting ferroptosis resistance in vivo and regulating GEM chemoresistance in PDAC tumor.

## Discussion

Postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy for PDAC typically consists of GEM-based treatment regimens, which is considered the gold standard in the field.. The primary mechanism of action of GEM is that it stimulates cancer cells to undergo the processes of apoptosis and ferroptosis. However, the majority of patients eventually exhibit drug resistance, significantly limiting patient prognosis. As a result, it is essential to better understand the mechanism behind GEM chemotherapeutic resistance. According to the findings of this investigation, exosomes from co-induced CAFs (co-cultured with PDAC cells and induced by GEM) were key regulators of GEM resistance in PDAC. First, we confirmed that native CAF exosomes did not inhibit GEM-induced ferroptosis and improve survival in PDAC cells. Next, we found that exosomes from co-induced CAFs inhibited GEM-induced ferroptosis in PDAC cells. More importantly, outside of co-culture with cancer cells, CAF exosomes lost the ability to promote GEM resistance in cancer cells. In the co-induced CAF exosomes, we found that miR-3173–5p targeted ACSL4. Following treatment with chemotherapy, we subsequently validated the increase of miR-3173–5p in pancreatic cancer tissue sections. Overall, we demonstrated that CAF exosome-derived miR-3173–5p sponged ACSL4 and suppressed ferroptosis, both of which are essential mechanisms of GEM resistance in PDAC.

Since receiving FDA approval of in 1996, the nucleoside analog of deoxycytidine known as GEM has been used extensively in the treatment of a wide range of solid cancers. It is the standard treatment for several types of cancers, including breast, ovarian, and non-small cell lung cancer.. The use of GEM in pancreatic cancer is limited because of the high level of resistance. Ferroptosis is a recently characterized iron-dependent programmed cell death process. Ferroptosis has been shown to be implicated in GEM chemotherapeutic resistance in a range of tumor types, including PDAC, according to research that was conducted recently.. Our study enriches the data in this field. ACSL4 is a key regulator of ferroptosis through the activation of fatty acid metabolism. Our data showed that GEM chemoresistance in PDAC cells was greatly enhanced after the knockdown of ACSL4 expression. Recent studies have suggested that ACSL4 is involved in mediating chemoresistance by inhibiting ferroptosis in the liver and prostate cancer. Using the TCGA database, we found that ACSL4 had low expression in pancreatic cancer and was positively correlated with the 5-year survival of pancreatic cancer patients. Our work is the first to reveal in vitro and in a xenograft tumor model that ACSL4-mediated suppression of ferroptosis is a crucial factor in GEM resistance in PDAC.

Recent research has demonstrated that TME is an important factor in determining the chemosensitivity of a tumor.. The TME is populated mostly by CAFs and mesenchymal stromal cells (MSC). Exosomes secreted from mesenchymal cells may be beneficial for normal tissue repair, but in cancer condition, these exosomes may modulate immune microenvironment that promote cancer invasion. Pancreatic cancer is a solid tumor full of stroma; therefore, CAFs in pancreatic cancer plays a pivotal role in tumor chemoresistance. Current studies have revealed that CAFs are functionally and molecularly heterogeneous in pancreatic cancer. It has been established in the vast majority of studies that CAFs substantially increase the growth of tumors and treatment resistance.. However, recent studies suggested that specific subsets of CAFs exhibit cancer-inhibitory abilities. Our results showed that native CAF exosomes did not affect GEM resistance in PDAC cells. By contrast, in mimicking 5-day chemotherapy in tumors, CAFs secreted exosomes that significantly enhanced GEM resistance in PDAC cells. Interestingly, the GEM resistance induced by CAF-secreted exosomes was lost when CAFs were treated with GEM chemotherapy alone. Cancerous growths are made up of cancer cells and TME, which together make up a multifaceted and ever-changing entity. The influence of TME on tumor cell chemotherapy resistance has been studied extensively; however, insufficient research has been conducted on the influence that tumor cells have on the TME. Our study demonstrated that cancer cells and the TME interact and jointly mediate the chemoresistance of pancreatic cancer. Richards et al. suggested that CAFs in PDAC have innate GEM resistance. Moreover, after GEM exposure, CAFs significantly enhanced PDAC chemotherapy resistance by secreting exosomes. Our study does not support the theory of innate resistance of CAF-derived exosomes. We observed that after GEM exposure, CAFs secreted exosomes that enhanced PDAC chemoresistance. However, our study showed that the ability of CAFs to resist chemotherapy requires the non-contact induction of tumor cells. The differences in the study results may be due to the heterogeneity of CAFs. Our results indicated that for a short period after chemotherapy, there is communication between tumor cells and the TME, which in turn induces non-intrinsic resistance of the TME to chemotherapy.

Exosomes are an important mode for maintaining communication between cancer cells, stromal cells, and immune cells in TME. Noncoding RNAs are the main effectors of the regulatory ability of exosomes. Exosomes produced from CAFs have been proven in studies to be capable of inducing cancer cell invasion, raising the risk of cancer cells to develope resistance to treatment, suppressing immune cell function, and priming distant organs for metastasis. The results of this study suggest a previously unknown mechanism of exosomal miRNAs in regulating chemoresistance in pancreatic cancer. miR-3173–5p has been shown to be capable of inhibiting tumor growth, and it inhibits tumor progression by sponging the ETS-related gene (ERG) and cytokine signaling 3 (SOCS3). Our study expands the role of miR-3173–5p in tumor regulation and shows that exosome-derived miR-3173–5p mediated GEM resistance in pancreatic cancer by sponging ACSL4. These findings are informative and could be applied for cancer precision medicine diagnosis, prognosis and therapeutics.

However, our study had limitations. The mechanism of how CAF exosomes regulate GEM resistance in pancreatic cancer identified in this study was derived from chemotherapy models, although it was validated in an in vitro and a xenograft mouse model. Further validation of this mechanism in GEM-resistant pancreatic cancer patients is needed. Enrollment of patients with GEM chemotherapy-resistant pancreatic cancer into a human trial has already begun. CAFs in xenograft tumors are mouse-derived cells, and their response to GEM chemotherapy may be different from that of primary tumors. Following GEM chemotherapy, the mechanism of high expression of miR-3173–5p by CAF exosomes and the communication between CAFs and cancer cells require further study. As a consequence of the complexity of tumor metabolism and the communication between tumor cells and the TME after chemotherapy, further study is necessary before implementing these discoveries into the therapeutic area.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, our findings demonstrated that CAFs play an active role in promoting GEM chemoresistance in pancreatic cancer through secretion of exosomes. The results of our study emphasize the need for further understanding the mechanisms of ferroptosis in chemotherapeutic resistance. Our research adds to the growing body of evidence suggesting that inhibiting the packaging of certain miRNAs into exosomes is an effective strategy for increasing cancer cell susceptibility to chemotherapy.
